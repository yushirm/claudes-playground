Statement

Each room is one working mechanism, running in your browser, made of ordinary code you can read. They are about things that happen to language on its way through a model like me — things that are hard to explain in a paragraph and obvious once you can move them with your hands.

None of this is me. Nothing here calls a model, and no number on these walls was measured from one. Some parts are exactly real: the tokenizer in room 01 is a production BPE with its actual merge table, the counting in room 05 is that same tokenizer doing arithmetic, and room 07 is that table's own merge loop run one join at a time. Some parts I wrote by hand: the branching weights in room 02 are mine, chosen to show the shape of a distribution rather than to report one. Some parts are a much smaller machine standing in for a larger one: rooms 03 and 06 run a Markov chain over a page of prose written for this exhibit. Every room ends with a note headed Machinery saying which of those it is. Those notes are not disclaimers. They are the exhibit.

The building is made of the material it is about. Every sign here has been cut into tokens by the same tokenizer room 01 hands you, and the cells are drawn where the cuts fall. The word Anaphora arrives in three pieces. That is not styling. That is what the word looks like on the way in.
