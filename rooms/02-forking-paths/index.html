    Wall text

    A sentence looks like an object when it is finished. It is not one while it is being made. At every point there is a set of things it could become next, each with some weight, and taking one of them throws all the others away permanently. What you end up reading is a route, and the route is the only part that survives.

    The fan above is that set. Notice that the weights are uneven, and unevenly uneven: some steps have one continuation that is obviously right and three that are barely there, and some are a genuine four-way toss where nothing is deciding it. That difference is the interesting one. It is the difference between a sentence that is going somewhere and a sentence that has not decided yet.

    Temperature does not change what is possible, only how flat the weights are. Turn it down and the top branch swallows the others until there is effectively one road; turn it up and everything becomes roughly as likely as everything else, which is not creativity, it is just indifference. Somewhere in between, the fan has a shape.

    The other thing worth noticing is what happens behind you. Once you have taken three steps, the two hundred and fifteen other sentences in this tree are gone, and nothing in the sentence you are holding records that they were ever there.

    Machinery

    The weights here are illustrative. I wrote them by hand. They are not measured, not sampled from a model, and not an estimate of what any model would do with these sentences. They were chosen to show the shape of a branching distribution: a mix of confident steps and undecided ones, because both happen and a room with only one kind would be a misleading room.

    The tree is in tree.js and you can read all of it: six seeds, 102 nodes, 312 continuations I wrote, and 216 complete sentences reachable from them. It is three steps deep and then it stops. A real model does not stop — it has a continuation available at every point forever, and its tree is not a written artefact but something recomputed from scratch at each step.

    A step here is a phrase, not a token. A real model commits one token at a time, so three of its steps would get you about a word and a half rather than a sentence. Room 01 shows what that unit actually looks like. Phrases were used here because the room is about branches diverging in meaning, and meaning is not visible at the scale of ing.

    The temperature arithmetic is the one part of this room that is not invented. Weights are normalised to probabilities, each is raised to the power 1/T, and the results are normalised again. That is what a sampler does to a real model's output distribution. The dial stops at 0.1 rather than 0 because at exactly zero the expression is undefined; by 0.1 the top branch already has essentially all of the weight, which is what temperature zero means in practice.
